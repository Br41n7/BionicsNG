# BionicsNG

**Open research into locally producible prosthetics and bionic technology in Nigeria.**

BionicsNG is an open research initiative exploring how Nigeria can progressively develop the knowledge, supply chains, manufacturing capacity, software, and engineering skills needed to produce affordable prosthetic and bionic technologies.

The project is in its **research and validation stage**. It does not claim to have a clinically validated device, a complete local manufacturing pipeline, or verified domestic sources for every component.

## Why this project exists

Prosthetic and bionic devices can be expensive and difficult to maintain when essential parts, specialist services, or replacement components depend on overseas supply chains. BionicsNG aims to investigate where local capability already exists, where adaptation or local production may be feasible, and where imports or international partnerships remain necessary.

The project welcomes contributors from prosthetics and orthotics, mechanical engineering, materials science, electronics, embedded systems, software, rehabilitation, manufacturing, research, and related fields.

## Initial objectives

1. Map Nigeria's prosthetics, rehabilitation, research, and manufacturing ecosystem.
2. Identify local materials, fabrication processes, workshops, laboratories, suppliers, and relevant expertise.
3. Decompose a representative EMG-controlled upper-limb prosthesis into subsystems and components.
4. Assess each component's realistic localization pathway: local design, local assembly, local fabrication, domestic component manufacture, or domestic raw-material processing.
5. Publish evidence, uncertainties, cost drivers, and research gaps so others can verify and improve the work.

## Research principles

- **Evidence before claims:** provide sources, dates, methods, and limitations.
- **Distinguish capability levels:** assembly is not the same as manufacturing a component or processing its raw material.
- **Label uncertainty:** use the project's evidence classifications rather than presenting assumptions as facts.
- **Safety and dignity:** engineering demonstrations are not automatically safe for clinical use.
- **Open collaboration:** welcome corrections, criticism, and contributions from Nigeria and internationally.
- **Practical affordability:** consider repairability, maintenance, supply reliability, and total lifecycle cost—not only initial build cost.

## Contributing

Contributions are welcome, including source verification, literature reviews, translations, supplier or laboratory leads, cost comparisons, CAD, simulation, software, and careful engineering critique.

1. Read [`CONTRIBUTING.md`](CONTRIBUTING.md) and the research methodology.
2. Search existing issues and documents to avoid duplicating work.
3. For substantial work, open an issue first to agree on scope.
4. Cite sources and clearly label unverified information.
5. Submit a pull request describing what changed, the evidence behind it, and remaining limitations.

Do not upload private personal information, confidential business information, patient records, or material you do not have permission to share.

## Safety notice

BionicsNG is an open research project, not a medical-device certification body or clinical service. Information and prototypes in this repository are provided for research and educational purposes. Do not use an unvalidated design on a person or represent it as clinically safe or effective. Human testing, patient use, and clinical claims require appropriate professional oversight, risk management, testing, ethics review where applicable, and compliance with relevant laws and regulations.
