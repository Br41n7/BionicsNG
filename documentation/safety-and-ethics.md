# Safety and Ethics

## Purpose

Prosthetic and bionic systems interact with the human body and can create mechanical, electrical, thermal, skin-contact, ergonomic, and psychological risks. Safety must be considered from the start, not added after a demonstration.

## General rules

- Repository designs are research and educational material unless explicitly assessed otherwise.
- A moving prototype is not evidence of clinical safety, reliability, or effectiveness.
- Do not fit, attach, or use an unvalidated prototype on a person.
- Do not perform human-subject research without appropriate ethical review, informed consent, professional oversight, and applicable approvals.
- Avoid collecting identifiable health or patient data. If such data is ever necessary, establish lawful authority, consent or another valid basis, secure handling, access controls, and retention rules before collection.
- Document foreseeable hazards, intended use, excluded uses, failure modes, and test limitations.

## Engineering considerations

Depending on the design, evaluate pinch and crush points, excessive force, actuator runaway, power loss, battery and charging hazards, overheating, electrical faults, material degradation, sharp edges, skin-contact irritation, contamination, entrapment, fatigue failure, and loss of control. Risk controls must be appropriate to the actual design and use environment.

## Clinical and regulatory boundaries

BionicsNG is not a clinical provider or regulatory authority. Before any device is offered for clinical use, the responsible team must determine applicable Nigerian and other relevant requirements, including device classification, quality management, risk management, verification and validation, clinical evaluation, documentation, post-market responsibilities, and professional oversight. Requirements depend on the device and intended use; obtain qualified advice rather than assuming a single checklist applies to every project.

## Reporting unsafe designs

If you identify a safety issue, describe the hazard, conditions, evidence, severity, and possible mitigation. Do not conceal failures to protect a project or contributor's reputation. Pause work when an unresolved hazard could reasonably cause harm.
