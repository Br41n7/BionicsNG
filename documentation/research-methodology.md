# Research Methodology

## Purpose

Make BionicsNG findings traceable, reproducible where possible, and honest about uncertainty.

## Research workflow

1. Define a specific question and scope.
2. Search primary sources first where available: standards, official institutional pages, peer-reviewed papers, patents, technical reports, procurement records, and manufacturer documentation.
3. Record the source, author or organization, date, access date, URL/DOI, and the exact claim supported.
4. Compare independent sources where the claim is important.
5. Record methods, assumptions, units, geography, time period, and limitations.
6. Assign an evidence classification.
7. Have another contributor review high-impact claims when possible.
8. Update or correct the record when new evidence appears.

## Evidence classifications

- **Verified:** directly supported by a reliable primary source or reproducible observation, within the stated scope. State what was verified; do not overgeneralize.
- **Reported:** a source makes the claim, but independent confirmation or supporting detail is incomplete. Attribute the claim to that source.
- **Hypothesis:** a plausible proposition requiring investigation or testing.
- **Unknown:** available evidence is insufficient, conflicting, inaccessible, or absent.

These labels describe the status of a particular claim, not the overall quality of a person or organization.

## Source quality

Prefer sources with identifiable authorship, methods, dates, and accountability. An official page can verify that an organization advertises a capability; it may not verify actual production volume, performance, certification, or supply capacity. News reports are useful leads but should be corroborated for technical and manufacturing claims. Social posts and promotional material should normally be treated as reported claims unless supported by stronger evidence.

## Local production classification

For each component or material, distinguish:

1. Local research or design.
2. Local assembly using imported components.
3. Local fabrication from purchased stock or feedstock.
4. Domestic manufacture of the component.
5. Domestic processing of raw material.
6. Supply-chain depth and origin of key inputs, where known.

Do not label a product “fully locally manufactured” unless the claim is clearly defined and supported by evidence.

## Cost research

Record currency, date, quantity, taxes, shipping, exchange-rate assumptions, import duties where known, tooling, labour, consumables, maintenance, expected service life, and replacement availability. Compare like with like. Separate quoted prices from estimates and label estimates explicitly.

## Conflicting evidence

Preserve material disagreements rather than silently choosing one source. Explain which source is stronger and why, and identify what would resolve the uncertainty.
